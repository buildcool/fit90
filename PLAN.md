## 2. Why This Works

11.8 kg in 92 days is 0.9 kg per week — aggressive but reachable because you held 64.2 kg at 13.1% BF in Apr 2025. Muscle memory means you regain strength and muscle far faster than a first-timer, so you can run a real deficit without losing the 32 kg of lean mass you already carry.

Maintenance (TDEE) at 80 kg with a 2-hour lift and 8-10k steps is roughly 2,600-2,700 kcal. Eating 1,750-1,950 kcal leaves a 700-850 kcal deficit — that is the whole fat-loss engine. Training does not burn the fat; the deficit does. Training is what makes sure the weight you lose is fat and not muscle.

Protein is set at 1.9-2.1 g per kg of bodyweight, which is the top of the useful range. On a pure-veg diet in a deficit this is the single hardest number to hit and the single most important one — miss it and you lose muscle instead of fat.

APT is corrected on a parallel track: it is not a weight problem, it is a 'tight hip flexors + weak glutes + weak deep core from 8 hours of sitting' problem. Every single gym day carries a dedicated APT block, and two of the six days are built around it.

### Phase 1 — Re-Activation — Weeks 1-2 (days 1-14)

Relearn every machine with perfect form, set your baseline weights, wake up the glutes and deep core.

- **Training:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7, 75s rest, tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Load rule:** Pick a weight you could do 4 more reps with. Write it down. This fortnight is for grooving the movement, not for ego.
- **Nutrition:** 1950 kcal · 150g protein · 200g carbs · 55g fat · 3.5L water
- **Cardio:** 8,000 steps daily. No extra machine cardio yet — let your joints adapt to 6 lifting days first.
- **APT focus:** Learn the posterior pelvic tuck. Supine pelvic tilts and deadbugs every single day, gym or not.
- **Expect:** 2.0-2.5 kg down, mostly water and glycogen in week 1. Do not panic when week 2 is slower.

### Phase 2 — Build & Burn — Weeks 3-6 (days 15-42)

The main fat-loss block. Add a fourth set, push loads up weekly, add post-lift cardio.

- **Training:** 4 sets × 10-12 @ RPE 8, 90s rest, tempo 1s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Load rule:** Double progression: when you hit the top of the rep range on all 4 sets, add 2.5 kg (upper body) or 5 kg (lower body) next session.
- **Nutrition:** 1850 kcal · 155g protein · 180g carbs · 52g fat · 3.5L water
- **Cardio:** 10,000 steps daily + 12 minutes easy cross-trainer or treadmill incline walk after each lift.
- **APT focus:** Full Release → Stretch → Strengthen → Re-pattern protocol daily. Hip flexor stretch is now non-negotiable every night.
- **Expect:** 0.8-1.0 kg per week. Clothes change before the scale does — track your waist.

### Phase 3 — Deload & Diet Break — Week 7 (days 43-49)

Planned recovery. This is not a week off — it is what lets Phase 4 work. Joints, CNS and hormones reset.

- **Training:** 2 sets × 12 @ RPE 5-6, 90s rest, tempo controlled
- **Load rule:** Drop every weight to roughly 60% of what you used in week 6. It should feel almost too easy. That is the point.
- **Nutrition:** 2150 kcal · 150g protein · 260g carbs · 55g fat · 3.5L water
- **Cardio:** Keep steps at 10,000. Skip the post-lift cardio entirely this week.
- **APT focus:** Mobility only — release and stretch work daily, lighter on the strengthening.
- **Expect:** The scale may stall or tick up 0.5 kg from extra carbs/water. It is not fat. Phase 4 drops fast because of this week.

### Phase 4 — Intensify — Weeks 8-11 (days 50-77)

Heaviest block. Lower reps, heavier loads, longer rest — this is what protects your muscle in a deep deficit.

- **Training:** 4 sets × 8-10 @ RPE 8-9, 120s rest, tempo 1s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Load rule:** Beat last week's logged number on the first set of every exercise, even by 1 rep. The app shows you what to beat.
- **Nutrition:** 1800 kcal · 160g protein · 165g carbs · 50g fat · 4L water
- **Cardio:** 10,000 steps daily + 15 minutes post-lift cardio + the full 45-60 min evening walk.
- **APT focus:** Load the correction — weighted hip thrusts and long-hold RKC planks. Your resting posture should visibly change in this block.
- **Expect:** 0.9-1.1 kg per week. Abs and the pelvic tilt both start showing around week 10.

### Phase 5 — Peak & Reveal — Weeks 12-13 (days 78-92)

Final push to 68.2 kg and 12%. Hold strength, strip the last fat, lock the new posture in as default.

- **Training:** 4 sets × 6-8 + drop set on the last set @ RPE 9, 120s rest, tempo 1s up • 2s squeeze • 3s down
- **Load rule:** Keep the loads of week 11 — do not chase PRs in the deepest deficit. On the final set, drop 30% and rep to failure.
- **Nutrition:** 1750 kcal · 165g protein · 150g carbs · 48g fat · 4L water
- **Cardio:** 12,000 steps daily + 20 minutes post-lift cardio. This is where the last 2 kg comes from.
- **APT focus:** Re-pattern only — the drills should now feel automatic. Re-shoot your standing side photo against week 1.
- **Expect:** 0.8-1.0 kg per week. Final check-in on day 92: 68.2 kg, 12% BF, neutral pelvis.

## 5. Sleep, Recovery & Cardio

### Sleep — 10:00 PM - 5:00 AM (7 hours)

- 7 hours is the floor, not the target. Under 6 hours and your body preferentially burns muscle instead of fat in a deficit — it is that direct. Sleep is a training variable.
- No caffeine after 6:00 PM. Half of a 4 PM coffee is still in your blood at 10 PM.

## 6. All Seven Training Days

Sets, reps, RPE and rest below are shown for **Phase 1 (weeks 1-2)**. They change automatically each phase — see section 3 for the progression, and the app shows the current prescription for whatever day you open.

### Day 1: Push — Monday

**Focus:** Chest, Shoulders & Triceps

Your heaviest upper-body pressing day. Pressing is also the number one trigger for Anterior Pelvic Tilt, because the easy way to press more weight is to arch your lower back. Every single press today is a chance to either feed the APT or fix it.

#### Warm-Up *(10 min)*

*Specific, never generic. You are raising tissue temperature and waking the shoulder blades up so the pressing has a stable base.*

**1. Treadmill Brisk Walk**

- **Machine:** Treadmill
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 5 min @ 5.5 km/h, 4% incline · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Set 5.5 km/h with a 4% incline. Hands off the rails.
- **Execution:** Walk tall for 5 minutes. You want to be slightly warm and breathing a little deeper, not tired.
- **APT cue:** Walk with your ribs stacked over your pelvis rather than your chest puffed and lower back arched. Start the posture work in the first 5 minutes of the day.
- **Common mistake:** Holding the rails and leaning back, which switches the whole thing off.
- **Substitute:** Cross-trainer or stationary bike, same 5 minutes

**2. Band Pull-Aparts**

- **Machine:** Resistance Band
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 20 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hold a light band at shoulder width, arms straight out in front at chest height.
- **Execution:** Pull the band apart until your arms are wide, squeezing your shoulder blades together. Return slowly. 20 reps.
- **APT cue:** Stand with your tailbone slightly tucked and glutes lightly squeezed. Do not let the pull-apart turn into a lower-back arch.
- **Common mistake:** Shrugging your shoulders up to your ears instead of pulling the blades together and down.
- **Substitute:** Light cable face pulls, or reverse pec deck with the lightest plate

**3. Scapular Wall Slides**

- **Machine:** Flat Wall
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 10 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Stand with your back, head and both forearms flat against a wall, elbows bent at 90 degrees.
- **Execution:** Slide your forearms up the wall overhead without letting them lift off, then slide back down. 10 slow reps.
- **APT cue:** Your entire lower back must stay flat against the wall for all 10 reps. If it lifts away, bend your knees slightly and tuck your pelvis. This is a live APT test disguised as a warm-up.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the lower back peel off the wall so the arms can go higher.
- **Substitute:** Cable or band overhead shrug, 2 x 12

**4. Light Chest Press Primer**

- **Machine:** Cult Fuel Chest Press
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 15 reps (very light) · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Same machine setup as your working sets, but with roughly 40% of your working weight.
- **Execution:** 15 smooth reps feeling the chest, not the shoulders. This grooves the exact movement pattern you are about to load.
- **APT cue:** Set your position now: ribs flat into the pad, glutes squeezed, lower back neutral. Memorise it before the weight gets heavy.
- **Common mistake:** Skipping this because it feels pointless — it is what makes set 1 as good as set 3.
- **Substitute:** Push-ups on knees, 2 x 12

#### Main Lifts & Accessories *(55 min)*

*Log every set. Beating last week's number on these seven movements is what protects your muscle while you lose 11.8 kg.*

**1. Incline Chest Press**

- **Machine:** Cult Fuel Series Chest Press
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Adjust the seat so the handles line up with your upper chest, just below the collarbone. Sit back and pull both shoulder blades down and together against the pad, then squeeze your glutes to lock your pelvis in place.
- **Execution:** Drive the handles forward and slightly up until your arms are almost straight, stopping just short of locking the elbows. Squeeze the chest for one second at the top. Take a full three seconds to let the handles come back until you feel a stretch across the chest.
- **APT cue:** Your lower back and bottom ribs stay pressed flat into the pad for every single rep. The moment you arch your lumbar spine off the seat to move more weight, you are training the exact posture you are here to fix.
- **Common mistake:** Setting the seat too low, which turns an incline chest press into a shoulder press and hammers your front delts instead of your chest.
- **Substitute:** Incline dumbbell press on an adjustable bench at 30 degrees

**2. Flat Dumbbell Press**

- **Machine:** Flat Bench + Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Lie on a flat bench with both feet planted firmly on the floor. Start with the dumbbells directly above your chest, elbows tucked to about 45 degrees from your torso — not flared straight out to the sides.
- **Execution:** Lower both dumbbells slowly until they are level with your chest and you feel a deep stretch across the pecs. Press back up and in, as if trying to touch the two dumbbells together without actually clanging them, and squeeze.
- **APT cue:** Exhale as you press and actively pull your bottom ribs down towards your hips. There should be no gap you could slide a hand into between your lower back and the bench.
- **Common mistake:** Flaring the elbows to 90 degrees, which puts all the load on the shoulder joint and is the single most common cause of gym shoulder pain.
- **Substitute:** Flat machine chest press, or push-ups with a slow 3-second lower

**3. Machine Shoulder Press**

- **Machine:** Cult Shoulder Press Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Set the seat so the handles sit at ear or jawline height. Grip the handles so your wrists stack directly above your elbows. Plant your feet and squeeze your glutes before the first rep.
- **Execution:** Press straight overhead until your arms are almost fully extended, keeping your head neutral. Lower under strict control back to chin level over three seconds.
- **APT cue:** This is the number one APT trigger in the entire gym. When the weight gets heavy your body will try to lean back and arch your lower spine to turn it into an incline press. Squeeze your glutes hard on the seat and glue your lower back to the backrest. If you cannot press it without arching, the weight is too heavy — drop it.
- **Common mistake:** Leaning back and arching the lower back to grind out the last two reps. Those two reps cost you more in posture than they buy you in muscle.
- **Substitute:** Seated dumbbell shoulder press on a bench set to 85 degrees

**4. Pec Deck Chest Fly**

- **Machine:** Cult Pec Deck Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Adjust the seat so the handles sit at mid-chest height. Sit tall with your shoulder blades pinned back and down against the pad.
- **Execution:** Bring the handles together in a wide smooth arc, like hugging a barrel. Squeeze your inner chest hard for one second when they meet, then let them travel back slowly until you feel the stretch.
- **APT cue:** Keep your ribs down and your lower back flat against the backrest for the whole set. The stretch at the end should be felt in your chest, not in your lower back.
- **Common mistake:** Bending the elbows more and more as the set gets hard, which quietly turns the fly into a press.
- **Substitute:** Cable crossover at chest height, or flat dumbbell fly with light weight

**5. Dumbbell Lateral Raise**

- **Machine:** Light Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Stand with a light dumbbell in each hand at your sides, a slight bend in the elbows, tailbone lightly tucked.
- **Execution:** Lead with your elbows and raise both arms out to the sides until they are level with your shoulders, no higher. Pause for a second, then lower over three seconds. Pretend you are pouring water out of two jugs.
- **APT cue:** Stand tall with your glutes lightly squeezed. Do not swing by rocking your hips forward and arching your lower back — if you need to swing, the dumbbells are too heavy.
- **Common mistake:** Going too heavy and shrugging. This is a 5-8 kg exercise for almost everyone, not a 15 kg one.
- **Substitute:** Cable lateral raise, single arm, or the lateral raise machine

**6. Rope Tricep Pushdown**

- **Machine:** Cable Crossover Machine (Rope)
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Attach a rope to the top pulley. Stand one step back with feet shoulder-width apart and knees slightly unlocked. Lean forward about 10 degrees from the hips.
- **Execution:** Pin your elbows to your ribs and keep them there. Push the rope down until your elbows are locked out, then pull the two rope ends apart and slightly back for one second. Return slowly without letting your elbows drift forward.
- **APT cue:** Stand with your tailbone tucked slightly under. Do not stick your butt backwards into an arched lower-back posture to get leverage.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the elbows drift forward and away from the ribs, which turns it into a chest press and takes the triceps out of it.
- **Substitute:** Straight-bar pushdown, or dumbbell kickbacks

**7. Overhead Rope Tricep Extension**

- **Machine:** Cable Machine (Low/Mid Pulley + Rope)
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Set the rope on a low or mid pulley. Face away from the machine in a staggered stance, rope held behind your head, elbows pointing forward and up alongside your ears.
- **Execution:** Extend your forearms forward and overhead until your arms are straight, squeeze, then let your hands travel back behind your head to feel a deep stretch in the long head of the triceps.
- **APT cue:** Anything overhead pulls your ribs up and your lower back into an arch. Tuck your ribs down hard and squeeze your glutes before the first rep, and keep the staggered stance — the stretch should be in your triceps, never in your lower spine.
- **Common mistake:** Arching the lower back to get the arms further overhead. Reduce the range instead and keep the pelvis neutral.
- **Substitute:** Single dumbbell overhead extension with both hands

#### APT Correction Block *(20 min)*

*The part everyone skips and the reason your posture never changes. You just spent 55 minutes pressing, which shortens hip flexors and tempts the lumbar into extension. This block pays that back immediately.*

**1. Supine Pelvic Tilt**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15 reps (3s hold) · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor, arms by your sides.
- **Execution:** Exhale fully, tighten your lower abs and glutes, and roll your pelvis backwards so your lower back presses completely flat into the mat. Hold for three seconds, then release slowly. 15 reps.
- **APT cue:** This is the single most important movement in the entire 90 days. You are teaching your nervous system what neutral actually feels like. Memorise the sensation and replicate about 30% of this tuck whenever you stand or walk.
- **Common mistake:** Pushing with your feet instead of rolling with your abs and glutes. Your heels should stay light.
- **Substitute:** Standing pelvic tilt against a wall

**2. Deadbug**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12 reps / side · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Lie on your back with both arms reaching straight at the ceiling and knees bent 90 degrees directly over your hips.
- **Execution:** Crush your lower back flat into the floor first. Then slowly extend your right arm back overhead and your left leg out straight at the same time, breathing out. Return and switch sides. 12 per side.
- **APT cue:** If your lower back lifts even one centimetre off the mat, you have gone too far — exhale harder and shorten the leg extension. The back staying flat is the entire exercise; the arms and legs are just the challenge.
- **Common mistake:** Rushing and holding your breath. Slow and exhaling is what makes the deep core fire.
- **Substitute:** Heel slides with the lower back pinned flat

**3. Glute Bridge**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15-20 reps (2s hold) · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Lie on your back with knees bent and heels about 6 inches from your glutes, arms flat by your sides.
- **Execution:** Flatten your lower back into the mat first, then drive through your heels and lift your hips until knees, hips and shoulders form one straight line. Hold the squeeze for two seconds at the top, then lower slowly.
- **APT cue:** The lift comes 100% from squeezing your glutes with your ribs staying down — never from pushing with your lower back. At the top you should feel it entirely in your glutes. If you feel it in your lower back, you have gone too high and are arching rather than extending the hip.
- **Common mistake:** Going as high as possible by hyperextending the lumbar spine. Stop at the straight line.
- **Substitute:** Hip thrust with your upper back on a bench

#### Cardio Finisher *(0-20 min (phase dependent))*

*Zero in weeks 1-2. 12 min from week 3. 15 min from week 8. 20 min in the last two weeks. Conversational pace only — this is for calories, not for suffering.*

**1. Incline Treadmill Walk**

- **Machine:** Treadmill
- **Prescription:** 1 set × Per phase (0-20 min) · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** 5-6 km/h at 8-10% incline. Hands off the rails.
- **Execution:** Walk at a pace where you could just about hold a conversation. Do not run — running after a lift in a deficit eats into your recovery for very little extra fat loss.
- **APT cue:** Walk tall with ribs stacked over pelvis and a light glute squeeze. Incline walking is genuinely good for your glutes if you do not lean on the rails.
- **Common mistake:** Gripping the rails and leaning back, which cuts the real effort roughly in half.
- **Substitute:** Cross-trainer or stationary bike at an easy resistance

#### Cool-Down & Stretch *(10 min)*

*Hip flexors last and longest — it is the stretch that matters most for your pelvis.*

**1. Doorway Pec Stretch**

- **Machine:** Doorway or Rack Upright
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 30 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Place your forearm flat on a doorframe with your elbow at shoulder height.
- **Execution:** Step through gently until you feel a stretch across the front of your chest. Hold 30 seconds, breathing out slowly. Switch sides.
- **APT cue:** Keep your ribs down and your pelvis neutral. The easy cheat here is to arch your back to feel a bigger stretch — that stretches your spine, not your chest.
- **Common mistake:** Yanking into it. Pressing muscles are warm and pliable now; be gentle.
- **Substitute:** Lying chest stretch over a foam roller

**2. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 60 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel on one knee with a folded mat under it, the other foot planted in front at 90 degrees. Torso vertical.
- **Execution:** Tuck your tailbone under into a posterior tilt FIRST, then squeeze the glute of the kneeling leg hard. Only then shift forward about two inches. Hold 60 seconds and breathe.
- **APT cue:** The tuck is the stretch. If you lean forward with an arched lower back you will feel a pull, but it is your lumbar spine compressing, not your psoas lengthening — and it makes the APT worse. Tuck, squeeze the glute, then move 2 inches. That is all the range you need.
- **Common mistake:** Lunging deep with an arched back and calling it a hip flexor stretch. This is the most commonly butchered stretch in any gym.
- **Substitute:** Couch stretch with the rear foot elevated on a bench

**3. Child's Pose with Side Reach**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 60 sec + 30 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel and sit back onto your heels, reaching your arms forward on the floor.
- **Execution:** Sink your hips back and breathe into your lower back for 60 seconds. Then walk both hands to the left for 30 seconds to open the right side, and repeat the other way.
- **APT cue:** This gently decompresses the lumbar erectors, which are chronically tight and short in APT and have been working hard all session.
- **Common mistake:** Holding your breath. The decompression comes from the exhale.
- **Substitute:** Seated forward fold

### Day 2: Pull — Tuesday

**Focus:** Back Width, Thickness, Rear Delts & Biceps

Pulling day builds the V-taper that makes 68 kg look lean, and it directly fights the rounded-forward desk posture you build up from 9 to 5. A strong upper back is what lets you sit tall without effort.

#### Warm-Up *(10 min)*

*Wake the lats and the mid-back up before you ask them to move weight, or your biceps and forearms will do the work instead.*

**1. Treadmill Brisk Walk**

- **Machine:** Treadmill
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 5 min @ 5.5 km/h, 4% incline · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** 5.5 km/h, 4% incline, hands off the rails.
- **Execution:** Five minutes to raise your temperature and heart rate before touching a weight.
- **APT cue:** Ribs stacked over pelvis, light glute squeeze, tall posture.
- **Common mistake:** Treating it as dead time on your phone instead of the start of the session.
- **Substitute:** Rowing machine, 5 min easy

**2. Band Pull-Aparts**

- **Machine:** Resistance Band
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 20 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Light band held at shoulder width, arms straight in front at chest height.
- **Execution:** Pull apart, squeeze the shoulder blades together, return slowly. 20 reps.
- **APT cue:** Tailbone slightly tucked, glutes lightly on. No lower-back arch to help.
- **Common mistake:** Shrugging up instead of squeezing back and down.
- **Substitute:** Reverse pec deck, lightest setting

**3. Scapular Pull-Ups**

- **Machine:** Pull-Up Bar / Assisted Pull-Up Machine
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 10 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hang from the bar with straight arms and a shoulder-width overhand grip. Use the assist pad if you need it.
- **Execution:** Without bending your elbows at all, pull your shoulder blades down and back so your body rises two or three inches. Hold one second, then let them rise again. 10 reps.
- **APT cue:** Keep a slight hollow-body position with ribs down rather than hanging in a big passive arch. Hanging with a relaxed arched back loads exactly the posture you are fixing.
- **Common mistake:** Bending the elbows and turning it into a tiny pull-up. The arms stay completely straight.
- **Substitute:** Dead hang for 20 seconds, 2 sets

**4. Light Lat Pulldown Primer**

- **Machine:** Cult Lat Pulldown Machine
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 15 reps (very light) · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** About 40% of your working weight, same grip you will use for your working sets.
- **Execution:** 15 smooth reps focusing on pulling your elbows down to your hips and feeling the lats, not the arms.
- **APT cue:** Lock in your position now: pelvis upright on the seat, ribs down, no leaning back.
- **Common mistake:** Skipping straight to a working weight with cold lats — the quickest way to a tweaked shoulder.
- **Substitute:** Light straight-arm pulldown, 2 x 15

#### Main Lifts & Accessories *(55 min)*

*On every pull, think 'elbows drive, hands just hold on'. If you feel it mostly in your forearms, you are pulling with your arms.*

**1. Lat Pulldown (Wide Overhand)**

- **Machine:** Cult Lat Pulldown Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Lock the thigh pad snugly over your quads so you cannot lift off the seat. Grip the wide bar slightly wider than shoulder-width, overhand. Sit upright with your pelvis stacked under your ribs.
- **Execution:** Pull your shoulders down away from your ears first, then drive your elbows down towards your hips until the bar touches your upper chest. Squeeze your lats for one second, then let the bar rise over three seconds until your lats are fully stretched.
- **APT cue:** Lean back no more than 10-15 degrees, and lean from your hips as one solid block rather than hinging at your lower spine. The classic error is rocking back and hyperextending the lumbar to drag the bar down — that is an APT rep, not a lat rep.
- **Common mistake:** Pulling the bar behind the neck. Bar to upper chest, always, in front.
- **Substitute:** Assisted pull-up machine, same rep range

**2. Seated Cable Row**

- **Machine:** Cult Seated Row Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Place your feet on the footplates with knees slightly bent. Sit tall with your chest up and grab the V-handle, arms long, feeling a stretch in your lats.
- **Execution:** Pull the handle into your lower stomach while squeezing your shoulder blades together hard for one second. Let your arms extend slowly over three seconds until you feel the stretch again.
- **APT cue:** Keep your pelvis upright on the seat and brace your abs as if someone is about to tap your stomach. Do not rock backwards to start the pull or let your lower back round forwards on the stretch — your torso should stay almost completely still.
- **Common mistake:** Using your whole torso as a rowing oar. If your body is swinging 30 degrees back and forth, the weight is too heavy.
- **Substitute:** Chest-supported machine row

**3. Single-Arm Dumbbell Row**

- **Machine:** Flat Bench + Dumbbell
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Place your left knee and left hand on a flat bench, right foot on the floor, right hand holding a dumbbell hanging straight down. Your back should be flat and parallel to the floor.
- **Execution:** Drive your right elbow up and back towards your hip, bringing the dumbbell to your ribs. Squeeze for one second, then lower over three seconds until your lat is fully stretched. Then switch sides.
- **APT cue:** Keep your spine in one straight neutral line from head to tailbone — no sag in the lower back, no rotation of the hips. Because you are bent over, this is a brilliant chance to practise holding neutral under load.
- **Common mistake:** Twisting your torso to get extra range. The shoulder moves, the spine stays still.
- **Substitute:** Chest-supported dumbbell row, or a single-arm cable row

**4. Straight-Arm Pulldown**

- **Machine:** Cable Machine (High Pulley + Rope/Bar)
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Stand facing a high pulley with a rope or straight bar, arms straight out in front at shoulder height, slight forward lean from the hips, knees unlocked.
- **Execution:** Keeping your elbows completely straight, sweep the bar down in an arc until it reaches your thighs. Squeeze your lats hard for one second, then let it rise back slowly.
- **APT cue:** Tailbone tucked slightly, glutes lightly squeezed, ribs down. Because you are leaning forward, your lower back will want to arch — do not let it.
- **Common mistake:** Bending the elbows, which turns a pure lat isolation into a weak pulldown.
- **Substitute:** Dumbbell pullover on a flat bench

**5. Face Pulls**

- **Machine:** Cable Machine (Eye-Level Rope)
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Set the pulley at eye level with a rope. Grab the rope with your thumbs pointing backwards and step back into a staggered stance.
- **Execution:** Pull the centre of the rope towards your forehead while rotating your wrists backwards into a double bicep pose. Squeeze your rear delts and upper back for one second. Return slowly.
- **APT cue:** Staggered stance, glutes squeezed, ribcage stacked directly over your pelvis. This is the single best exercise for undoing 8 hours of desk hunch, so it is worth doing properly rather than heavy.
- **Common mistake:** Going too heavy and turning it into a row to the chest. Light weight, high reps, rope to the forehead.
- **Substitute:** Reverse pec deck, or band pull-aparts at face height

**6. Dumbbell Bicep Curl**

- **Machine:** Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Stand tall with a dumbbell in each hand, palms facing forward, elbows pinned to your sides.
- **Execution:** Curl both dumbbells up through a full range, squeeze the biceps hard at the top, and lower over three full seconds until your arms are completely straight.
- **APT cue:** Squeeze your glutes so you never lean backwards and arch your lower back to heave the weight up. If your body is swinging, drop the weight by 2 kg — nobody has ever built biceps with momentum.
- **Common mistake:** Swinging the torso and only doing the top half of the rep. Full extension at the bottom is where biceps grow.
- **Substitute:** Cable curl, or the preacher curl machine

**7. Preacher / Incline Hammer Curl**

- **Machine:** Cult Preacher Curl Pad or Incline Bench
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Rest the backs of your upper arms flat on the preacher pad, or sit back on a 45-degree incline bench and let your arms hang straight down with a neutral grip.
- **Execution:** Curl up with your upper arms staying completely still on the pad, squeeze at the top, then lower over three seconds to a dead-straight arm.
- **APT cue:** Because your arms are fixed, this is one of the few exercises where you cannot cheat with your back — which is exactly why it is here at the end of the session when you are tired.
- **Common mistake:** Lifting the elbows off the pad to get past the hard part of the rep.
- **Substitute:** Seated incline dumbbell curl on a 45-degree bench

#### APT Correction Block *(20 min)*

*Anti-extension and glute work. Your upper back just worked hard; now lock the pelvis in underneath it.*

**1. RKC Plank**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 45-60 sec hold · rest 60s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Forearm plank with elbows directly beneath your shoulders and feet hip-width apart.
- **Execution:** Tuck your tailbone aggressively under into a posterior tilt, squeeze your glutes and quads at maximum force, and try to pull your elbows towards your toes. Breathe shallowly but do not hold your breath. 45-60 seconds of maximum tension.
- **APT cue:** This rewires your resting posture more directly than almost anything else, because it trains your glutes and abs to hold a posterior tilt against gravity. A normal relaxed 3-minute plank does almost nothing for APT; 45 brutal seconds of this does.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the belly and hips sag towards the mat, which is literally practising APT. If you can hold it past 60 seconds comfortably, you are not squeezing hard enough.
- **Substitute:** Standard forearm plank with an aggressive tailbone tuck

**2. Bird Dog**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 10 reps / side · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel on all fours with hands directly under your shoulders and knees directly under your hips. Back flat.
- **Execution:** Brace your core, then extend your right arm forward and left leg straight back until both are parallel with the floor. Hold two seconds, return, switch sides. 10 per side.
- **APT cue:** Do not lift the leg higher than hip height. Going higher feels more impressive but it comes entirely from arching your lower back, which trains the APT instead of fixing it. Imagine balancing a glass of water on your lower back the whole time.
- **Common mistake:** Rushing and rotating the hips open. Slow, square hips, leg no higher than hip level.
- **Substitute:** Dead bug, if wrists or knees complain

**3. Hip Thrust**

- **Machine:** Bench + Barbell or Glute Drive Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 reps (2s hold) · rest 60s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Rest your upper back, around the bottom of your shoulder blades, against a bench with a padded barbell across your hips, or use the glute drive machine. Feet flat, shins vertical at the top.
- **Execution:** Keep your chin tucked and eyes forward. Drive through your heels to lift your hips until your thighs are parallel to the floor and your body makes a straight line from knees to shoulders. Hold the squeeze two seconds, lower under control.
- **APT cue:** Chin tucked and ribs down is what forces the glutes to do the work instead of your lower back. At the top you should feel it 100% in the glutes. Strong glutes are what hold your pelvis in neutral all day — this is the strengthening half of the APT fix.
- **Common mistake:** Looking up at the ceiling and finishing with a big lower-back arch instead of a glute squeeze.
- **Substitute:** Bodyweight glute bridge on the floor, 3 x 20

#### Cardio Finisher *(0-20 min (phase dependent))*

*Same rules as Day 1. Easy pace, phase-dependent duration.*

**1. Cross-Trainer**

- **Machine:** Elliptical / Cross-Trainer
- **Prescription:** 1 set × Per phase (0-20 min) · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Moderate resistance, upright posture, light grip on the handles.
- **Execution:** Steady conversational pace for the phase duration.
- **APT cue:** Stand tall rather than hanging off the handles — slumping forward here just rehearses desk posture with your heart rate up.
- **Common mistake:** Cranking the resistance so high it becomes a leg workout the day before leg day.
- **Substitute:** Incline treadmill walk

#### Cool-Down & Stretch *(10 min)*

**1. Lat Stretch on Rack**

- **Machine:** Rack Upright or Bar
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 30 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hold a rack upright at waist height with one hand, then sit your hips back and away from it.
- **Execution:** Let your hips sink back and down until you feel a long stretch down the side of your back. Hold 30 seconds, breathing out. Switch sides.
- **APT cue:** Keep your ribs down and pelvis neutral rather than letting your lower back hollow out to deepen the stretch.
- **Common mistake:** Bouncing. Hold and breathe.
- **Substitute:** Child's pose with a side reach

**2. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 60 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel on one knee, other foot planted in front at 90 degrees, torso vertical.
- **Execution:** Tuck the tailbone FIRST, squeeze the kneeling-side glute, then shift forward two inches. 60 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Every day, both sides, no exceptions. Tuck first, then move — the tuck is what actually lengthens the psoas.
- **Common mistake:** Deep lunging with an arched back.
- **Substitute:** Couch stretch with the rear foot on a bench

**3. Thoracic Extension Opener**

- **Machine:** Foam Roller
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 10 reps + 30 sec hold · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Lie with a foam roller across your mid-back, hands supporting your head, knees bent.
- **Execution:** Extend gently back over the roller 10 times, then hold the last one for 30 seconds, breathing deeply.
- **APT cue:** Keep your ribs from flaring and your glutes lightly squeezed. You want the extension in your mid-back, not in your lower back — that distinction is the entire point.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the roller slide down to the lower back and cranking there. Mid-back only.
- **Substitute:** Doorway pec stretch

### Day 3: Legs — Wednesday

**Focus:** Glute Activation, Hamstrings & Quads

Legs are your biggest muscle group, so this is your highest calorie-burning session of the week. It is also APT work in disguise: every exercise here is chosen to strengthen glutes and hamstrings, which are the two muscles that pull your pelvis back into neutral.

#### Warm-Up *(10 min)*

*Never load a cold hip. Glutes in particular need waking up because sitting all day switches them off.*

**1. Stationary Bike**

- **Machine:** Upright Bike
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 5 min easy · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Seat height so your knee has a slight bend at the bottom. Light resistance.
- **Execution:** Five easy minutes to get blood into the quads, hamstrings and hips.
- **APT cue:** Sit upright on your sit bones instead of slumping onto your tailbone.
- **Common mistake:** Going hard and arriving at the leg press already tired.
- **Substitute:** Treadmill walk, 5 min at 4% incline

**2. Bodyweight Glute Bridge**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 20 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, knees bent, heels close to your glutes.
- **Execution:** Flatten the lower back, then bridge up to a straight line and squeeze hard for one second. 20 reps.
- **APT cue:** This is your glute activation. After 8 hours of sitting your glutes are effectively asleep, and if you go straight into a leg press they stay asleep while your quads and lower back take over.
- **Common mistake:** Skipping it and wondering why you feel leg day in your lower back.
- **Substitute:** Standing glute squeezes, 2 x 20

**3. Walking Knee Hugs & Leg Swings**

- **Machine:** Open Floor
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 10 / side each · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Stand tall with space in front of you.
- **Execution:** Walk forward hugging one knee to your chest per step for 10 per side. Then hold a rack and swing each leg forward and back 10 times, then side to side 10 times.
- **APT cue:** Keep your standing pelvis level and glute lightly squeezed instead of letting your lower back arch as the leg swings back.
- **Common mistake:** Forcing the range with momentum. Controlled swings, growing gradually.
- **Substitute:** Standing hip circles, 10 per side

**4. Bodyweight Squat**

- **Machine:** Bodyweight
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 15 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out, arms forward for balance.
- **Execution:** Squat down as far as you can with a tall chest and a neutral spine, then stand and squeeze your glutes at the top. 15 reps.
- **APT cue:** At the top, finish by squeezing your glutes and tucking your tailbone slightly, not by pushing your hips forward into an arch.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the knees cave in. Push them out over your toes.
- **Substitute:** Light goblet squat, 2 x 12

#### Main Lifts & Accessories *(55 min)*

*Glutes and hamstrings are biased deliberately over quads. That is both the aesthetic you want at 68 kg and the fix for your pelvis.*

**1. Leg Press (High Foot Placement)**

- **Machine:** Cult Leg Press Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 120s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Sit firmly against the backrest with your hips all the way back. Place your feet shoulder-width apart on the UPPER half of the platform — this biases glutes and hamstrings rather than just quads.
- **Execution:** Disengage the safety bars, lower the sled slowly until your knees reach about 90 degrees, then drive through your heels to press back up without locking your knees out hard at the top.
- **APT cue:** Never lower so deep that your tailbone curls off the seat pad. The moment your pelvis tucks under and your lower back rounds away from the pad, you are loading your lumbar discs instead of your legs. Keep your sacrum glued down and stop the rep an inch above where that happens.
- **Common mistake:** Chasing depth. Your safe range ends where your pelvis starts moving, and that is non-negotiable regardless of what the person next to you is doing.
- **Substitute:** Hack squat, or goblet squats if the press is taken

**2. Romanian Deadlift (Dumbbells)**

- **Machine:** Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 120s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Stand tall holding dumbbells against the front of your thighs. Unlock your knees to about a 15-degree bend and keep them there for the whole set.
- **Execution:** Push your hips straight backwards towards the wall behind you while sliding the dumbbells down the front of your shins. Go until you feel a strong hamstring stretch, roughly mid-shin, then drive your hips forward and squeeze your glutes to stand up.
- **APT cue:** Your spine stays in one neutral line throughout — the movement is entirely at the hip. At the top, finish by squeezing your glutes and stopping at full hip extension; do not lean backwards past standing, which is just an arched lumbar finish. This is the best hamstring and glute builder you have, and strong hamstrings pull the back of your pelvis down into neutral.
- **Common mistake:** Turning it into a squat by bending the knees, or rounding the lower back at the bottom to reach further down. Range is limited by your hamstring flexibility, not your willpower.
- **Substitute:** Seated or lying leg curl if your lower back is fatigued

**3. Leg Extension**

- **Machine:** Cult Leg Extension Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Adjust the backrest so the backs of your knees sit flush against the seat edge, with the ankle pad resting just above your feet.
- **Execution:** Grip the side handles, extend your legs fully until straight, hold the quad squeeze for one second, then lower over three seconds.
- **APT cue:** Pull yourself down into the seat using the side handles so your pelvis stays flat and neutral. Do not let your hips lift off the seat as you extend — that is your lower back helping and it defeats the purpose.
- **Common mistake:** Slamming into full extension with heavy weight. Controlled, with a pause at the top.
- **Substitute:** Goblet squat, or sissy squat

**4. Lying Hamstring Curl**

- **Machine:** Cult Lying Leg Curl Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Lie face down with your kneecaps just clear of the bench edge and the roller pad resting on your lower calves. Hold the handles.
- **Execution:** Curl the pad up towards your glutes, pause one second at peak contraction, and lower slowly over three seconds without letting the weight crash down.
- **APT cue:** CRITICAL FOR APT: press your hips and pubic bone down into the pad before every single rep. If your butt rises as you curl, your lower back is doing the work and you are reinforcing the arch. Hips down, hamstrings up.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the hips pop up at the top of every rep, which is the single most common error on this machine.
- **Substitute:** Seated leg curl, or dumbbell RDL at lighter weight

**5. Bulgarian Split Squat**

- **Machine:** Dumbbells + Flat Bench
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 10 reps / leg @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Stand about two feet in front of a flat bench and rest the top of your back foot on it. Hold a dumbbell in each hand.
- **Execution:** Lower your back knee towards the floor with a slight 15-degree forward torso lean to load the front glute. Drive up through your front heel. 10 reps, then switch legs.
- **APT cue:** This is a two-for-one: the front glute is strengthening while the rear leg's hip flexor gets an active stretch under load, which is one of the most effective ways to lengthen a tight psoas. Keep your ribs down and your tailbone slightly tucked so the rear hip actually gets the stretch.
- **Common mistake:** Standing too close to the bench, which crushes the front knee and removes the hip flexor stretch. Two feet of space minimum.
- **Substitute:** Walking lunges, or a static split squat holding the rack

**6. Hip Abduction**

- **Machine:** Cult Hip Abduction Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Sit with the pads against the outside of your knees. Lean forward slightly from the hips, which biases the upper glute.
- **Execution:** Push your knees apart against the pads, hold the squeeze for one second at the widest point, then return slowly against the resistance.
- **APT cue:** Your glute medius, trained here, is what stops your pelvis dropping and rotating when you stand and walk. A weak glute medius shows up as a hip that drifts forward — directly related to your tilt.
- **Common mistake:** Using momentum and bouncing the pads open. Slow out, slow back.
- **Substitute:** Banded lateral walks, 3 x 20 steps each way

**7. Standing Calf Raise**

- **Machine:** Calf Raise Machine or Step + Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15-20 reps @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Balls of your feet on the platform edge, heels hanging free, legs straight.
- **Execution:** Drop your heels down for a deep stretch, then rise all the way onto your toes and squeeze for one second. Full range, 15-20 reps.
- **APT cue:** Stand tall with ribs stacked. Do not bounce by pumping your hips and lower back — the movement happens at the ankle only.
- **Common mistake:** Tiny bouncing half-reps. Full stretch at the bottom, full squeeze at the top.
- **Substitute:** Single-leg calf raise on a step holding a dumbbell

#### APT Correction Block *(20 min)*

*Your hip flexors just worked hard on every leg exercise. This block tilts the balance straight back towards the glutes and deep core.*

**1. Weighted Glute Bridge**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat + Plate/Dumbbell
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15 reps (2s hold) · rest 60s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, knees bent, heels close, with a plate or dumbbell held across your hips.
- **Execution:** Flatten the lower back first, then drive through the heels to a straight line and hold the squeeze for two seconds.
- **APT cue:** Ribs stay down. Stop at the straight line, never above it. If you feel this in your lower back rather than your glutes, you are hyperextending — reduce the height.
- **Common mistake:** Over-bridging into a lumbar arch to get the plate higher.
- **Substitute:** Bodyweight glute bridge, 3 x 25

**2. Supine Pelvic Tilt**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15 reps (3s hold) · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, knees bent, feet flat.
- **Execution:** Exhale, tighten lower abs and glutes, roll the pelvis back so the lower back presses completely flat. Hold three seconds. 15 reps.
- **APT cue:** After a heavy leg session your pelvis will want to sit in an exaggerated tilt. This resets it before you walk out of the gym.
- **Common mistake:** Pushing through the feet instead of rolling with the abs.
- **Substitute:** Standing wall pelvic tilt

**3. Deadbug**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12 reps / side · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, arms to the ceiling, knees bent 90 degrees over the hips.
- **Execution:** Press the lower back flat, extend opposite arm and leg slowly while breathing out, return, switch. 12 per side.
- **APT cue:** The deep core is what holds the pelvis where your glutes put it. Back flat at all times.
- **Common mistake:** Extending so far that the lower back lifts.
- **Substitute:** Heel slides with the back pinned flat

#### Cardio Finisher *(0-15 min (phase dependent))*

*Keep it short and flat after a heavy leg day — your legs are the thing that needs to recover for Day 6.*

**1. Flat Treadmill Walk**

- **Machine:** Treadmill
- **Prescription:** 1 set × Per phase (0-15 min) · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** 5 km/h, 0-2% incline only today.
- **Execution:** Easy flat walk to flush the legs. No incline after heavy leg work.
- **APT cue:** Tall posture, ribs over pelvis.
- **Common mistake:** Doing incline work here and trashing your legs before Day 6.
- **Substitute:** Easy stationary bike

#### Cool-Down & Stretch *(10 min)*

**1. Standing Quad Stretch**

- **Machine:** Rack for Balance
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 45 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hold a rack for balance, grab one ankle and pull your heel towards your glute.
- **Execution:** Tuck your tailbone under first, then pull the heel in. Hold 45 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** The tuck is what makes this reach the hip flexor rather than just the quad. Without it you will arch your back and feel almost nothing useful.
- **Common mistake:** Arching the lower back and letting the knee drift forward.
- **Substitute:** Side-lying quad stretch on the mat

**2. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 60 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel on one knee, other foot planted in front, torso vertical.
- **Execution:** Tuck tailbone, squeeze the kneeling glute, shift forward two inches. 60 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Longest and most important stretch of leg day. Tuck first, always.
- **Common mistake:** Deep lunge with an arched back.
- **Substitute:** Couch stretch

**3. Seated Hamstring Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 45 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Sit on the mat with one leg straight and the other bent, foot tucked in.
- **Execution:** Hinge forward from the hips with a flat back, reaching towards the toes of the straight leg. Hold 45 seconds, switch.
- **APT cue:** Hinge at the hip, do not round your lower back to reach further. Tight hamstrings is common in APT and lengthening them helps the pelvis sit level.
- **Common mistake:** Rounding the spine to touch the toes. Flat back, less range, better stretch.
- **Substitute:** Standing single-leg hamstring stretch on a low bench

**4. Figure-4 Glute Stretch**

- **Setup:** Lie on your back, cross your right ankle over your left knee to make a figure 4.

- **APT cue:** Keep your lower back flat on the mat as you pull the knee in — this is also a gentle check that your pelvis can sit neutral.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the hips roll and lifting the back off the mat.

### Day 4: Core & APT — Thursday

**Focus:** Anterior Pelvic Tilt Correction & Deep Core Bracing

The dedicated posture day, and the most important session of the week for how you will look and feel standing still. Nothing here is heavy. All of it is precise. This is the day that changes your resting posture, and it follows the full Release → Stretch → Strengthen → Re-pattern order for a reason — you cannot strengthen a muscle into a position that a tight muscle will not let it reach.

#### Warm-Up & Breathing Reset *(10 min)*

*Today starts with breathing, not with movement. Your diaphragm position and your pelvis position are directly linked.*

**1. Treadmill Brisk Walk**

- **Machine:** Treadmill
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 5 min @ 5.5 km/h · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** 5.5 km/h, 2-4% incline.
- **Execution:** Five minutes to warm up the tissue before mobility work. Cold stretching does very little.
- **APT cue:** Practise walking tall: ribs over pelvis, glutes lightly engaged, tailbone very slightly tucked.
- **Common mistake:** Stretching cold and wondering why nothing lengthens.
- **Substitute:** Cross-trainer, 5 min

**2. 90/90 Breathing**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat + Wall
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 5 breaths · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Lie on your back with your feet flat on a wall, hips and knees both bent at 90 degrees. Arms reaching towards the ceiling.
- **Execution:** Push gently into the wall with your heels, tuck your tailbone, and feel your lower back flatten. Breathe in through your nose for 4 seconds, then exhale fully through your mouth for 8 seconds, letting your ribs drop down. Five full breaths per set.
- **APT cue:** This is the foundation of the whole correction. In APT your ribs flare up and your diaphragm sits in a bad position, which makes a neutral pelvis almost impossible to hold. The long exhale physically pulls your ribcage down over your pelvis and switches your deep core on. If you only ever did one APT drill, it would be this one.
- **Common mistake:** Breathing into your chest and shoulders. The exhale should feel like your ribs are closing down like an umbrella.
- **Substitute:** Supine 90/90 with feet on a bench

**3. Cat-Camel**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 10 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On all fours, hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
- **Execution:** Slowly round your whole spine up towards the ceiling, tucking your tailbone, then slowly reverse into a gentle arch. 10 slow controlled reps through the full range.
- **APT cue:** Deliberately feel both ends of the range. Most people in APT genuinely cannot find the 'cat' end — the rounded, tailbone-tucked position. That missing end of the range is your problem, so spend longer there.
- **Common mistake:** Rushing. Three seconds in each direction minimum.
- **Substitute:** Standing pelvic tilts

#### Release & Stretch (Step 1 + 2) *(20 min)*

*Open the tight structures first. Trying to strengthen your glutes while your hip flexors are locked short is like pulling a rope that is tied to a wall.*

**1. Foam Roll Quads & Hip Flexors**

- **Machine:** Foam Roller
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 90 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Lie face down with the roller under the front of one thigh, high up near the hip.
- **Execution:** Roll slowly from just above the knee up to the front of the hip. When you find a tender spot, stop there and breathe for 20-30 seconds until it eases. 90 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Your rectus femoris and psoas have been held short for 8 hours a day for years. Rolling does not permanently lengthen them, but it does reduce the tone enough that the stretch that follows actually works. Release, then stretch — that order matters.
- **Common mistake:** Rolling fast like a rolling pin. Slow, and pause on the sore spots.
- **Substitute:** Massage ball or a water bottle

**2. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 60 sec / side · rest 15s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel on one knee with a folded mat underneath, other foot planted in front at 90 degrees, torso completely vertical.
- **Execution:** Step 1: tuck your tailbone under hard into a posterior tilt. Step 2: squeeze the glute of the kneeling leg at full force — this reciprocally relaxes the hip flexor. Step 3: only now shift forward about two inches. Hold 60 seconds breathing out slowly. Three rounds per side.
- **APT cue:** The most important stretch of the entire 90 days, done here three times per side. The order is everything: tuck, squeeze, then move. Done this way you will feel it high in the front of the hip, which is the psoas. Done with an arched back you will feel it in the lumbar spine, which is useless and makes things worse.
- **Common mistake:** Leaning forward into a deep lunge. If you can go a long way forward, your pelvis is not tucked.
- **Substitute:** Couch stretch with the rear foot on a bench

**3. Couch Stretch**

- **Machine:** Bench or Wall
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 60 sec / side · rest 15s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel with your rear shin flat against a bench or wall so the knee is bent fully and the foot is up. Front foot planted.
- **Execution:** Tuck your tailbone, squeeze the rear glute, then bring your torso upright. Hold 60 seconds per side. This is an advanced progression — build into it over the weeks.
- **APT cue:** By bending the knee fully you add the rectus femoris to the stretch, which crosses both knee and hip and is a major contributor to the tilt that the standard stretch partly misses.
- **Common mistake:** Going straight to a fully upright torso. Start leaning forward and work up to vertical over several weeks.
- **Substitute:** Half-kneeling stretch with the rear knee further back

**4. Seated Hamstring Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 45 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Sit with one leg straight, the other bent with the foot tucked in.
- **Execution:** Hinge forward from the hip with a flat back, reaching towards the toes. 45 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Hinge at the hip with a neutral spine. Tight hamstrings often coexist with APT because they are being stretched all day by the tilt — they are long and tight rather than short and tight, so stretch them gently and prioritise strengthening them instead.
- **Common mistake:** Rounding the back to reach the toes.
- **Substitute:** Standing hamstring stretch with the foot on a bench

#### Strengthen & Re-pattern (Step 3 + 4) *(40 min)*

*Now that the front of the hip is open, teach the glutes and deep core to hold the new position. This block is the actual rewiring.*

**1. RKC Plank**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 4 sets × 45-60 sec hold · rest 60s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Forearm plank, elbows under shoulders, feet hip-width.
- **Execution:** Tuck the tailbone hard, squeeze glutes and quads at 100%, pull your elbows towards your toes. Breathe shallow but keep breathing. 45-60 seconds of maximum tension, four rounds.
- **APT cue:** This trains your glutes and abs to hold a posterior tilt against gravity, which is exactly the job they have to do when you stand all day. If you can hold it for two minutes, you are not generating enough tension — make 45 seconds brutal instead of making it longer.
- **Common mistake:** A long relaxed plank with a sagging belly, which is practising the problem.
- **Substitute:** Forearm plank with an aggressive tuck, or on knees

**2. Deadbug**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 4 sets × 12 reps / side · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, arms to the ceiling, knees bent 90 degrees over the hips.
- **Execution:** Crush the lower back flat, then extend opposite arm and leg slowly while exhaling. Return under control and switch. 12 per side, four rounds.
- **APT cue:** This is the anti-extension pattern that teaches your deep core to resist your lower back arching while your limbs move — which is the exact skill you need for walking, lifting and standing without tilting.
- **Common mistake:** Extending beyond the point where the back stays flat. Range follows control, not the other way round.
- **Substitute:** Heel slides, or dead bug with legs only

**3. Hip Thrust**

- **Machine:** Bench + Barbell or Glute Drive Machine
- **Prescription:** 4 sets × 12-15 reps (2s hold) · rest 90s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Upper back on a bench, padded bar across the hips, feet flat, shins vertical at the top.
- **Execution:** Chin tucked, drive through the heels to a straight knee-to-shoulder line, hold the glute squeeze two seconds, lower under control. Progressive: add weight weekly.
- **APT cue:** This is the single strongest glute builder available, and your glutes are the muscle that physically pulls the back of your pelvis down into neutral. Chin tucked and ribs down are what keep the work in the glutes instead of the lower back.
- **Common mistake:** Looking up and finishing with a lumbar arch rather than a glute squeeze.
- **Substitute:** Weighted glute bridge on the floor

**4. Cable Pallof Press**

- **Machine:** Cable Machine (Chest Height)
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12 reps / side · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Set the cable at chest height. Stand side-on to the machine, feet shoulder-width, holding the handle at your chest with both hands.
- **Execution:** Press the handle straight out in front of your chest and hold for two seconds while the cable tries to rotate you. Bring it back in. 12 per side.
- **APT cue:** Tailbone tucked and glutes squeezed throughout. This is anti-rotation work, and it trains the deep core in a standing position, which transfers directly to holding your pelvis neutral while you walk around all day.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the torso twist towards the machine, or standing with an arched back.
- **Substitute:** Band Pallof press anchored to a rack

**5. Reverse Crunch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat or Decline Bench
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15 reps · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Lie on your back with knees bent and hands by your hips or holding something behind your head.
- **Execution:** Curl your knees towards your chest by rolling your pelvis backwards — the movement comes from your pelvis tucking, not from swinging your legs. Lower slowly over three seconds.
- **APT cue:** This is the exact opposite of a sit-up. Sit-ups train hip flexors, which you want nothing to do with. The reverse crunch trains the lower abs to pull your pelvis into a posterior tilt — literally the muscle action that corrects your tilt.
- **Common mistake:** Swinging the legs with momentum. If your pelvis is not rolling, you are just doing leg raises.
- **Substitute:** Supine pelvic tilt with the legs raised

**6. Side Plank**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 40 sec / side · rest 45s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Lie on your side, elbow directly under your shoulder, feet stacked or staggered.
- **Execution:** Lift your hips so your body makes one straight line from head to feet. Squeeze the bottom glute. 40 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Trains the obliques and glute medius together, which is the pair that keeps your pelvis level from side to side. Squeeze the bottom glute and keep your ribs down rather than letting your hips sag or rotate.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the hips drop or rolling forwards onto the chest.
- **Substitute:** Side plank from the knees

**7. Standing Wall Posture Reset**

- **Machine:** Flat Wall
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 45 sec hold · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Stand with your heels about 10 cm from a wall and your head, shoulder blades and glutes all touching it.
- **Execution:** Slide one hand behind your lower back. Now tuck your tailbone and squeeze your glutes until your hand is pinned snugly. Hold that for 45 seconds, breathing out long. Then step away from the wall and try to keep exactly that feeling for 10 steps.
- **APT cue:** This is the re-patterning step, and it is the one that makes the other six days stick. All the strength in the world does nothing if your brain's default standing position is still a tilt. Finish every APT session by walking away holding the corrected position — that is how a drill becomes a posture.
- **Common mistake:** Doing the hold and then immediately slumping as you walk off. The 10 steps afterwards are the actual exercise.
- **Substitute:** Standing posture reset without a wall, in front of a mirror

#### Cardio Finisher *(15-20 min)*

*Longer today because the strength work is lighter. Use it to practise walking in your corrected posture.*

**1. Incline Treadmill Walk**

- **Machine:** Treadmill
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 15-20 min · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** 5-6 km/h at 8-10% incline, hands off the rails.
- **Execution:** Walk for 15-20 minutes. Every two minutes, consciously reset: ribs down, tailbone slightly tucked, glutes lightly on.
- **APT cue:** Incline walking with a tucked pelvis is glute training that also burns calories. This is re-patterning practice with your heart rate up, which makes it stick faster.
- **Common mistake:** Holding the rails, which lets your hips drift back into a tilt.
- **Substitute:** Outdoor brisk walk

#### Cool-Down *(10 min)*

**1. Child's Pose with Side Reach**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 60 sec + 30 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel, sit back on your heels, arms reaching forward.
- **Execution:** Sink the hips back and breathe into the lower back for 60 seconds, then walk the hands to each side for 30 seconds.
- **APT cue:** Decompresses the chronically tight lumbar erectors, which in APT are short and overworked from holding the arch all day.
- **Common mistake:** Holding your breath.
- **Substitute:** Seated forward fold

**2. Figure-4 Glute Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 45 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, right ankle crossed over the left knee.
- **Execution:** Pull the left knee to your chest, feel the right glute stretch. 45 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Keep the lower back flat on the mat throughout.
- **Common mistake:** Rolling the hips and lifting the back.
- **Substitute:** Seated figure-4 in a chair

**3. 90/90 Breathing (Closing)**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat + Wall
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 5 breaths · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Feet on the wall, hips and knees at 90 degrees.
- **Execution:** Tuck the tailbone, 4-second nasal inhale, 8-second mouth exhale, ribs dropping. Five breaths, twice.
- **APT cue:** Finish the day the way you started it. Leaving the gym in a rib-down, pelvis-neutral position is what carries the work into the next 23 hours.
- **Common mistake:** Skipping it because the session already felt done.
- **Substitute:** Supine breathing with knees bent

### Day 5: Upper Body — Friday

**Focus:** Calisthenics Return & Full Upper Hypertrophy

A push-pull combined day that doubles as your return to bodyweight strength. As you drop from 80 kg to 68 kg, your pull-ups will improve dramatically for free — every kilo you lose is a kilo you no longer have to pull. By week 13, unassisted pull-ups are a realistic target.

#### Warm-Up *(10 min)*

**1. Rowing Machine**

- **Machine:** Rower
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 5 min easy · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Feet strapped, light damper setting.
- **Execution:** Five easy minutes. The rower warms up both the pushing and pulling muscles at once, which suits a combined day.
- **APT cue:** Hinge from your hips with a neutral spine on each stroke rather than rounding or over-arching your lower back.
- **Common mistake:** Yanking with the arms and rounding the back.
- **Substitute:** Treadmill walk, 5 min

**2. Band Pull-Aparts**

- **Machine:** Resistance Band
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 20 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Light band at shoulder width, arms straight ahead.
- **Execution:** Pull apart, squeeze the blades, return slowly. 20 reps.
- **APT cue:** Tailbone tucked, glutes lightly on.
- **Common mistake:** Shrugging instead of squeezing.
- **Substitute:** Reverse pec deck, lightest setting

**3. Scapular Pull-Ups**

- **Machine:** Pull-Up Bar
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 10 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hang with straight arms, shoulder-width overhand grip.
- **Execution:** Without bending the elbows, pull the shoulder blades down so your body rises a couple of inches. 10 reps.
- **APT cue:** Hold a slight hollow body with ribs down rather than hanging in a passive arch.
- **Common mistake:** Bending the elbows.
- **Substitute:** Dead hang, 2 x 20 sec

**4. Push-Up to Downward Dog**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat

- **Setup:** Start in a push-up position with hands under your shoulders.
- **Execution:** Do one controlled push-up, then push your hips up and back into downward dog, pressing your heels towards the floor. Return. Eight reps.
- **APT cue:** Keep your ribs down in the push-up position so you are not hanging in an arch. The downward dog opens up your hamstrings and calves while the push-up primes your chest.
- **Common mistake:** Sagging hips in the push-up portion.
- **Substitute:** Knee push-ups, 2 x 10

#### Main Lifts & Accessories *(55 min)*

*Alternate push and pull so each muscle group rests while the other works. This lets you move through eight exercises inside the time.*

**1. Assisted Pull-Ups**

- **Machine:** Cult Assisted Pull-Up Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 8-10 reps @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Select counterweight assistance — start at 20-25 kg of help at 80 kg bodyweight. Kneel securely on the pad with an overhand grip slightly wider than shoulder-width.
- **Execution:** Hang with arms fully extended, pull your shoulder blades down first, then drive your elbows towards your ribs until your chin clears the bar. Lower over three full seconds.
- **APT cue:** Keep your abs braced in a slight hollow-body position rather than letting your lower back arch and your legs swing forward. Reduce the assistance by 2.5 kg whenever you can complete 10 clean reps — by week 13 at 68 kg you should be close to unassisted.
- **Common mistake:** Kipping and swinging to get the chin over. A clean assisted rep beats a sloppy unassisted one every time.
- **Substitute:** Lat pulldown at a weight that limits you to 8-10 reps

**2. Flat Dumbbell Press**

- **Machine:** Flat Bench + Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Flat bench, feet planted, dumbbells above the chest with elbows at 45 degrees from the torso.
- **Execution:** Lower slowly until level with the chest, feeling the pec stretch, then press up and in and squeeze.
- **APT cue:** Pull your bottom ribs down towards your hips so there is no gap under your lower back.
- **Common mistake:** Flaring elbows to 90 degrees.
- **Substitute:** Machine chest press

**3. Seated Cable Row**

- **Machine:** Cult Seated Row Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Feet on the plates, knees slightly bent, sit tall, V-handle in hand.
- **Execution:** Pull to the lower stomach, squeeze the blades together for one second, extend slowly.
- **APT cue:** Pelvis upright, abs braced, torso almost still. No rowing-oar swinging.
- **Common mistake:** Using the whole torso to move the weight.
- **Substitute:** Chest-supported machine row

**4. Seated Dumbbell Shoulder Press**

- **Machine:** Adjustable Bench (85°) + Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Set the bench to nearly vertical at 85 degrees. Sit with feet planted and dumbbells at shoulder height, elbows angled about 30 degrees forward rather than straight out to the sides.
- **Execution:** Press overhead until your arms are almost straight, keeping the dumbbells in the scapular plane. Lower under control to ear level.
- **APT cue:** Press your lower back into the bench pad and squeeze your glutes BEFORE every rep. The 85-degree bench instead of a fully vertical one exists specifically so you can keep your lumbar flat. Overhead pressing is the biggest APT trap in the gym.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the lower back arch off the pad on the last few reps.
- **Substitute:** Machine shoulder press

**5. Pec Deck Chest Fly**

- **Machine:** Cult Pec Deck Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Seat at mid-chest height, shoulder blades pinned back.
- **Execution:** Wide arc together, squeeze the inner chest one second, return slowly to a stretch.
- **APT cue:** Ribs down, lower back flat against the backrest.
- **Common mistake:** Progressively bending the elbows into a press.
- **Substitute:** Cable crossover at chest height

**6. Face Pulls**

- **Machine:** Cable Machine (Eye-Level Rope)
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15 reps @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Pulley at eye level, rope, thumbs pointing back, staggered stance.
- **Execution:** Pull the rope centre to your forehead while rotating into a double bicep pose. Squeeze one second.
- **APT cue:** Glutes squeezed, ribcage stacked over the pelvis. Best single exercise for undoing desk hunch.
- **Common mistake:** Going heavy and turning it into a row.
- **Substitute:** Reverse pec deck

**7. Assisted Dips**

- **Machine:** Cult Assisted Dip Machine or Parallel Bars
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 8-12 reps @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Set the assistance counterweight and kneel on the pad. Grip the bars with a slight forward torso lean to bias the chest.
- **Execution:** Lower until your upper arms are roughly parallel to the floor, then press back up and squeeze. Stop short of a hard lockout.
- **APT cue:** Hold a slight hollow body with ribs down. Do not let your legs swing forward and your lower back arch, which is the standard dip cheat.
- **Common mistake:** Going too deep and stressing the shoulder joint. Upper arms parallel is deep enough.
- **Substitute:** Bench dips with feet on the floor, or close-grip push-ups

**8. Superset: Hammer Curl + Rope Pushdown**

- **Machine:** Dumbbells + Cable Rope
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12 reps each, back to back @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Set the dumbbells next to the cable station so you can move between them with no rest.
- **Execution:** 12 hammer curls with a neutral grip, then immediately 12 rope pushdowns, then rest 60 seconds. That is one set. This saves time and gives a strong arm pump at the end of the session.
- **APT cue:** Stand tall with glutes squeezed for both halves. By the end of the session you are tired and that is exactly when the lower back starts helping — do not let it.
- **Common mistake:** Swinging both movements because you are fatigued. Drop the weight and keep the form.
- **Substitute:** Any curl paired with any tricep extension

**2. Lat Stretch on Rack**

- **Machine:** Rack Upright
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 30 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hold the upright at waist height and sit your hips back.
- **Execution:** Sink back until you feel the stretch down the side of your back. 30 seconds each side.
- **APT cue:** Ribs down, pelvis neutral.
- **Common mistake:** Hollowing the lower back to deepen it.
- **Substitute:** Child's pose with side reach

**3. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 60 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel on one knee, other foot forward, torso vertical.
- **Execution:** Tuck, squeeze the glute, shift forward two inches. 60 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Every day, both sides. Tuck first.
- **Common mistake:** Deep lunge with an arched back.
- **Substitute:** Couch stretch

### Day 6: Legs & APT — Saturday

**Focus:** Posterior Chain & Hip Flexor Lengthening

The second leg day, built entirely around the posterior chain — glutes and hamstrings — and around exercises that stretch the hip flexors while loading them. If Day 3 was the heavy leg day, this one is the posture-fixing leg day. It is arguably the most valuable session of your week for the tilt.

#### Warm-Up *(10 min)*

**1. Stationary Bike**

- **Machine:** Upright Bike
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 5 min easy · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Light resistance, seat set so the knee has a slight bend at the bottom.
- **Execution:** Five easy minutes to get blood into the hips and legs.
- **APT cue:** Sit upright on the sit bones, not slumped back onto the tailbone.
- **Common mistake:** Going hard and pre-fatiguing your legs.
- **Substitute:** Treadmill walk at 4% incline

**2. Bodyweight Glute Bridge**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 20 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, knees bent, heels close.
- **Execution:** Flatten the back, bridge up, squeeze one second. 20 reps.
- **APT cue:** Mandatory glute wake-up before you load the hips.
- **Common mistake:** Skipping it.
- **Substitute:** Standing glute squeezes

**3. Leg Swings (Front-Back, Side-Side)**

- **Machine:** Rack for Support
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 10 / direction / side · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hold a rack upright for balance.
- **Execution:** Swing one leg forward and back 10 times, then across your body side to side 10 times. Switch legs.
- **APT cue:** Keep the standing hip level and the glute lightly squeezed. Do not let your lower back arch as the leg swings behind you — that is the swing turning into an APT rep.
- **Common mistake:** Forcing range with momentum.
- **Substitute:** Standing hip circles

**4. Bodyweight Goblet Squat Pattern**

- **Machine:** Bodyweight or Light Dumbbell
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 12 reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hold a light dumbbell at your chest, feet shoulder-width.
- **Execution:** Squat down between your hips with a tall chest, 12 controlled reps, squeezing the glutes at the top.
- **APT cue:** Finish each rep with a glute squeeze and a slight tailbone tuck instead of pushing the hips forward into an arch.
- **Common mistake:** Knees caving in.
- **Substitute:** Bodyweight squat

#### Main Lifts & Accessories *(55 min)*

*Hinge-dominant. Every exercise here either strengthens what pulls your pelvis back to neutral, or stretches what pulls it forward.*

**1. Goblet Squat**

- **Machine:** Heavy Dumbbell or Kettlebell
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Hold a dumbbell vertically against your chest like a goblet with your elbows pointing down. Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out.
- **Execution:** Sit straight down between your hips, keeping your chest tall and your knees tracking out over your toes. Go as deep as you can without your pelvis tucking under, then drive straight back up and squeeze your glutes.
- **APT cue:** Holding the weight at the front works as a counterbalance that naturally keeps your pelvis neutral and your core engaged — which is exactly why a goblet squat is a better choice than a back squat for someone correcting APT. A barbell on your back encourages the arch; a weight at your chest fights it.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the elbows drop forward and the chest collapse, which rounds the lower back at the bottom.
- **Substitute:** Leg press with high foot placement, or hack squat

**2. Romanian Deadlift (Dumbbells)**

- **Machine:** Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 120s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Stand tall with dumbbells against the front of your thighs, knees unlocked to about 15 degrees.
- **Execution:** Push your hips straight back while sliding the dumbbells down your shins. Stop at a strong hamstring stretch around mid-shin, then drive the hips forward and squeeze the glutes hard to stand.
- **APT cue:** The single best exercise in this plan for your tilt. Strong hamstrings and glutes pull the back of your pelvis down into neutral, and the RDL trains exactly that under load. Finish standing tall with glutes squeezed, never leaning back past vertical.
- **Common mistake:** Bending the knees into a squat, or rounding the back at the bottom to reach lower. The hamstring stretch sets your range, nothing else.
- **Substitute:** Barbell RDL, or seated leg curl if the lower back is tired

**3. Bulgarian Split Squat**

- **Machine:** Dumbbells + Flat Bench
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 10 reps / leg @ RPE 6-7 · rest 90s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Stand two feet in front of a bench with the top of your back foot resting on it. Dumbbell in each hand.
- **Execution:** Lower the back knee towards the floor with a slight forward torso lean to load the front glute, then drive up through the front heel. 10 reps per leg.
- **APT cue:** The best two-for-one in the plan: the front glute strengthens while the rear hip flexor is stretched under load, which is one of the most effective ways to lengthen a stubborn psoas. Keep your ribs down and tailbone slightly tucked so the rear hip actually gets the stretch rather than your lower back taking it.
- **Common mistake:** Standing too close to the bench, which kills both the depth and the stretch.
- **Substitute:** Reverse lunges, or a static split squat

**4. Barbell Hip Thrust**

- **Machine:** Bench + Barbell or Glute Drive Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 reps @ RPE 6-7 · rest 120s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Upper back against a bench at the bottom of the shoulder blades, padded barbell across the hips, feet flat with shins vertical at the top.
- **Execution:** Chin tucked, eyes forward. Drive through the heels until your thighs are parallel to the floor and your body is a straight line from knees to shoulders. Hold two seconds, lower under control.
- **APT cue:** This is where you add real load to the correction. Chin tucked and ribs down force 100% glute activation; look up at the ceiling instead and your lower back takes it. Add weight every week — strong glutes under load are what hold your pelvis neutral for 16 waking hours.
- **Common mistake:** Over-extending at the top into a lumbar arch instead of finishing with a glute squeeze.
- **Substitute:** Glute drive machine, or weighted glute bridge

**5. Lying Hamstring Curl**

- **Machine:** Cult Lying Leg Curl Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 12-15 @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Face down, kneecaps just off the bench edge, roller on the lower calves.
- **Execution:** Curl towards the glutes, pause one second, lower over three seconds.
- **APT cue:** Press your hips and pubic bone down into the pad before every rep. If your butt lifts as you curl, your lower back is doing the work.
- **Common mistake:** Hips popping up at the top of each rep.
- **Substitute:** Seated leg curl

**6. Hip Abduction**

- **Machine:** Cult Hip Abduction Machine
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15-20 reps @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Pads against the outside of the knees, slight forward lean from the hips.
- **Execution:** Push the knees apart, hold one second at the widest, return slowly against the resistance.
- **APT cue:** Glute medius keeps your pelvis level side to side. Weak here and your pelvis drifts and rotates, which compounds the forward tilt.
- **Common mistake:** Bouncing with momentum.
- **Substitute:** Banded lateral walks, 3 x 20 steps each way

**7. Standing Calf Raise**

- **Machine:** Calf Machine or Step + Dumbbells
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15-20 reps @ RPE 6-7 · rest 75s · tempo 2s up • 1s squeeze • 3s down
- **Setup:** Balls of the feet on the platform edge, heels free, legs straight.
- **Execution:** Deep stretch at the bottom, full rise onto the toes, one-second squeeze. 15-20 reps.
- **APT cue:** Stand tall with ribs stacked. The movement is at the ankle only — no hip pumping.
- **Common mistake:** Short bouncy half reps.
- **Substitute:** Single-leg calf raise on a step

#### APT Correction Block — Lengthening Focus *(20 min)*

*Your hip flexors have been loaded and stretched all session, which makes them maximally receptive now. This is the best 20 minutes of your week for actually lengthening them.*

**1. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 60 sec / leg · rest 15s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel on one knee with a folded mat underneath, other foot planted in front at 90 degrees, torso vertical.
- **Execution:** Tuck the tailbone under hard. Squeeze the kneeling-side glute at full force. Only then shift forward two inches. Hold 60 seconds, breathing out long. Three rounds per side.
- **APT cue:** Three full rounds per side, today of all days, because the tissue is warm and has just been loaded through a stretch on the split squats. Tuck, squeeze, then move — in that order, every time.
- **Common mistake:** Leaning forward into a lunge with an arched back, which compresses your lumbar spine and does nothing for your psoas.
- **Substitute:** Couch stretch with the rear foot on a bench

**2. Couch Stretch**

- **Machine:** Bench or Wall
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 60 sec / side · rest 15s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Rear shin flat against a bench or wall so the knee is fully bent, front foot planted.
- **Execution:** Tuck the tailbone, squeeze the rear glute, bring the torso upright. 60 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** The bent knee adds the rectus femoris, which crosses both the hip and the knee and is a major contributor to the tilt that the standard stretch only partly reaches.
- **Common mistake:** Going fully upright before you are ready. Progress the torso angle over weeks.
- **Substitute:** Half-kneeling stretch with the rear knee set further back

**3. Supine Pelvic Tilt**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 15 reps (3s hold) · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, knees bent, feet flat.
- **Execution:** Exhale, tighten lower abs and glutes, roll the pelvis back so the lower back flattens completely. Hold three seconds. 15 reps.
- **APT cue:** Now that the hip flexors are freshly lengthened, your pelvis can reach a true neutral. Use this window to practise finding it — it will feel noticeably easier than it did at the start of the session.
- **Common mistake:** Pushing with the feet.
- **Substitute:** Standing wall pelvic tilt

**4. 90/90 Breathing**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat + Wall
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 5 breaths · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Feet on the wall, hips and knees at 90 degrees, arms towards the ceiling.
- **Execution:** Heels press gently into the wall, tailbone tucked. Inhale 4 seconds through the nose, exhale 8 seconds through the mouth, ribs dropping down. Five breaths, three rounds.
- **APT cue:** Finishes the week by locking the ribs-over-pelvis position in. Do this and walk out of the gym holding it.
- **Common mistake:** Chest breathing instead of a full rib-dropping exhale.
- **Substitute:** Supine breathing with the knees bent

#### Cardio Finisher *(0-15 min (phase dependent))*

**1. Flat or Low-Incline Treadmill Walk**

- **Machine:** Treadmill
- **Prescription:** 1 set × Per phase (0-15 min) · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** 5 km/h, 0-4% incline.
- **Execution:** Easy walk to flush the legs after a hinge-heavy session.
- **APT cue:** Practise the corrected walking posture: ribs down, tailbone slightly tucked.
- **Common mistake:** Steep incline on tired hamstrings.
- **Substitute:** Easy bike

#### Cool-Down & Stretch *(10 min)*

**1. Standing Quad Stretch**

- **Machine:** Rack for Balance
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 45 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Hold the rack, grab one ankle, heel towards the glute.
- **Execution:** Tuck the tailbone first, then pull the heel in. 45 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** The tuck is what takes this into the hip flexor instead of leaving it at the quad.
- **Common mistake:** Arching the back and letting the knee drift forward.
- **Substitute:** Side-lying quad stretch

**2. Seated Hamstring Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 45 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** One leg straight, the other bent with the foot tucked in.
- **Execution:** Hinge forward from the hip with a flat back. 45 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Hinge at the hip, do not round the lower back.
- **Common mistake:** Rounding the spine to reach the toes.
- **Substitute:** Standing hamstring stretch with the foot on a bench

**3. Figure-4 Glute Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 45 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** On your back, one ankle crossed over the opposite knee.
- **Execution:** Pull the knee towards your chest until you feel the glute stretch. 45 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Keep the lower back flat on the mat.
- **Common mistake:** Letting the hips roll and the back lift.
- **Substitute:** Seated figure-4 in a chair

**4. Child's Pose**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 90 sec · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel and sit back on your heels, arms reaching forward.
- **Execution:** Sink the hips back, breathe into the lower back for 90 seconds.
- **APT cue:** Decompresses the lumbar erectors after a hinge-heavy day. End the training week relaxed, not braced.
- **Common mistake:** Rushing it. This is the last 90 seconds of your training week — use them.
- **Substitute:** Seated forward fold

### Day 7: Active Recovery — Sunday

**Focus:** Mobility, Weekly Check-In & Food Prep

Not a day off — a day that does a different job. Muscle is built while you recover, not while you train, and skipping this day is how people stall in week 5. No gym, no lifting, no 'quick session'. Walk, do the full mobility circuit unhurried, take your measurements, and set the next week up.

#### Weekly Check-In *(20 min)*

*Do this first thing, before eating, in the same clothes and light as last week. This is the data the entire plan steers by.*

**1. Weigh-In & Waist Measurement**

- **Machine:** Scale + Tape
- **Prescription:** 1 set × Once weekly · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** First thing after waking, after the toilet, before food or water, same clothes every week.
- **Execution:** Weigh yourself and log it in the Stats tab. Then measure your waist at navel height, relaxed, at the end of a normal exhale. Log that too.
- **APT cue:** Waist matters more than weight in weeks 4-7. The scale stalls when you simultaneously gain muscle and lose fat, but the tape keeps moving. When they disagree, trust the tape and the mirror.
- **Common mistake:** Weighing on random days at random times and reading meaning into noise. Same day, same time, same conditions, and judge the 7-day average.
- **Substitute:** Weight alone if you have no tape measure — but get a tape measure

**2. Progress Photo**

- **Machine:** Phone + Mirror
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 3 angles · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Same spot, same light, same time of day, minimal clothing, phone at hip height.
- **Execution:** Take a front, side and back photo. The side-on one is the critical one for APT — keep it strictly side-on and stand relaxed, in your natural posture, not in your corrected one.
- **APT cue:** Stand how you NORMALLY stand for the photo. Do not correct your posture for the camera or you destroy the only objective record you have of the tilt changing. Compare week 1 against week 13 side by side at the end.
- **Common mistake:** Flexing or fixing your posture, which makes the photos useless for tracking the tilt.
- **Substitute:** Front and side only if you are short on time

**3. APT Self-Test (Every 2 Weeks)**

- **Machine:** Wall + Floor
- **Prescription:** 1 set × All 4 tests · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Open wall space and a mat. Run this on weeks 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 and 13.
- **Execution:** Run all four tests from the APT tab — wall test, Thomas test, ASIS landmark test and the standing side photo — and log each result.
- **APT cue:** This is the only way you will know the APT work is doing anything. Posture changes too slowly to notice day to day, but the hand-behind-the-back gap in the wall test shrinks measurably every fortnight.
- **Common mistake:** Never re-testing, then guessing in week 10 whether it is working.
- **Substitute:** Wall test alone if time is short

#### Full APT Mobility Circuit *(25 min)*

*The one day you run the whole circuit without watching the clock. Full hold times, both sides, properly. Mobility only today — no strengthening.*

**1. Foam Roll Quads & Hip Flexors**

- **Machine:** Foam Roller
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 2 min / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Face down with the roller under the front of one thigh, up near the hip.
- **Execution:** Roll slowly from above the knee to the hip, pausing 20-30 seconds on tender spots. Two minutes per side today.
- **APT cue:** Six days of training have left your hip flexors loaded and short. Take the time today.
- **Common mistake:** Rolling fast. Slow, and pause where it bites.
- **Substitute:** Massage ball

**2. Foam Roll Glutes & Lower Back**

- **Machine:** Foam Roller
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 90 sec / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Sit on the roller with one ankle crossed over the opposite knee.
- **Execution:** Roll the glute slowly for 90 seconds per side. Then roll the upper and mid back, but never roll directly on your lower spine.
- **APT cue:** Roll the glutes and the mid-back, not the lumbar spine. In APT the lumbar erectors are already short and irritable, and grinding a roller into them tends to make them guard harder, not relax.
- **Common mistake:** Rolling directly on the lower back.
- **Substitute:** Tennis or lacrosse ball against a wall

**3. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 60 sec / side · rest 15s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Kneel on one knee, other foot planted in front, torso vertical.
- **Execution:** Tuck, squeeze the glute, shift forward two inches. 60 seconds. Three rounds per side.
- **APT cue:** Three rounds per side, unhurried. Tuck first, always.
- **Common mistake:** Deep lunging with an arched back.
- **Substitute:** Couch stretch

**4. Cat-Camel**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 2 sets × 12 slow reps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** All fours, hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
- **Execution:** Slowly round the whole spine up, tucking the tailbone, then slowly reverse. Three seconds each direction, 12 reps.
- **APT cue:** Spend extra time at the rounded, tailbone-tucked end of the range — that is the position your body has forgotten how to find.
- **Common mistake:** Going fast and only using the mid-back.
- **Substitute:** Standing pelvic tilts

**5. 90/90 Breathing**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat + Wall
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 6 breaths · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Feet on the wall, hips and knees at 90 degrees.
- **Execution:** Tailbone tucked, 4-second nasal inhale, 8-second mouth exhale with the ribs dropping. Six breaths, three rounds.
- **APT cue:** The rib-over-pelvis reset. Do it slowly today — three rounds, unhurried, is genuinely one of the highest-value things in this whole plan.
- **Common mistake:** Chest breathing.
- **Substitute:** Supine breathing with knees bent

**6. Figure-4 & Hamstring Stretch**

- **Machine:** Floor Mat
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 60 sec each / side · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Mat, on your back.
- **Execution:** Figure-4 glute stretch for 60 seconds per side, then seated hamstring stretch for 60 seconds per side.
- **APT cue:** Lower back flat on the mat for the figure-4; hinge from the hip with a flat back for the hamstring.
- **Common mistake:** Rounding the spine on the hamstring stretch.
- **Substitute:** Seated versions in a chair

**7. Standing Wall Posture Reset**

- **Machine:** Flat Wall
- **Prescription:** 3 sets × 45 sec hold + 10 steps · rest 30s · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Heels 10 cm from a wall, head, shoulder blades and glutes touching it.
- **Execution:** Slide a hand behind your lower back, then tuck the tailbone and squeeze the glutes until your hand is pinned. Hold 45 seconds. Step away and walk 10 steps holding exactly that position.
- **APT cue:** The last thing you do in the circuit, because the 10 steps afterwards are what turn a drill into your new default posture.
- **Common mistake:** Slumping the instant you step off the wall.
- **Substitute:** Mirror posture reset

#### Easy Recovery Walk *(30-45 min)*

*Blood flow for recovery, not a workout. Easy conversational pace, flat ground, no intervals.*

**1. Easy Outdoor Walk**

- **Machine:** Outdoors
- **Prescription:** 1 set × 30-45 min easy · tempo controlled
- **Setup:** Comfortable shoes, flat route, no incline.
- **Execution:** Walk 30-45 minutes at a genuinely easy pace where you could hold a full conversation. Target 6,000-8,000 steps across the whole day.
- **APT cue:** Use the walk as free re-patterning practice: every few minutes reset to ribs down, tailbone slightly tucked, glutes lightly engaged. Thousands of corrected steps a week is what makes the new posture automatic.
- **Common mistake:** Turning the recovery walk into a session. If you finish out of breath, you did it wrong.
- **Substitute:** Flat treadmill walk if the weather is bad

### When Life Gets In The Way

**Eating out with friends** — Order a paneer tikka (dry, not in gravy) or a tandoori platter plus a salad, and skip the naan, rice and sweet drink. Roughly 500 kcal and 30 g protein. Then eat lighter at your next meal, not for the next three days.

**PG food is unusually oily or there is nothing but fried food** — Eat the roti and the solids, skip the gravy completely, and make up the protein with a whey scoop and 50 g paneer. A deliberately imperfect meal beats a skipped one.

**You travelled and missed two meals** — Do not try to eat 1,000 kcal at 10 PM to 'hit your target'. Take a whey scoop, go to sleep, and eat normally tomorrow. One low day across 92 days is statistically invisible.

**You are genuinely starving at 11 PM** — 500 ml water first, wait 10 minutes. If you are still hungry, eat 60 g paneer or half a scoop of whey — protein, not carbs. Then look at whether your dinner was actually too small, because chronic late hunger means the plan needs adjusting, not more willpower.

**A birthday, festival or a meal you actually want** — Eat it, enjoy it properly, and go straight back to plan at the next meal. One 1,500 kcal meal costs you about two days of progress. Three weeks of guilt-driven chaos afterwards costs you the whole phase. Build it in instead of pretending it will not happen.

**You are sick** — Drop the training, keep the protein, eat at maintenance (around 2,600 kcal) until you recover. Trying to run a deficit while ill extends how long you are ill.

## 8. Supplements

### Whey Protein Isolate (essential)

- **Dose:** 1-2 scoops (25-50 g protein)
- **When:** 1 scoop immediately post-workout (7:00 AM). 2nd scoop wherever the day's protein falls short — usually pre-bed.
- **Why:** You need 150-165 g protein daily on a pure-veg diet. Food alone gets you to about 110 g without cooking. Whey closes the gap. It is a supplement in the literal sense — it supplements, it does not replace meals.

### Creatine Monohydrate (essential)

- **Dose:** 5 g, every single day
- **When:** Any time — pick a fixed anchor so you never forget. Stir into your post-workout shake.
- **Why:** The most studied supplement that exists. Adds 5-10% strength, which in a deficit is the difference between holding muscle and losing it. Take it on rest days too — it works by saturating your muscles over weeks, not by giving you a pre-workout kick. No loading phase needed.

### Vitamin B12 (essential)

- **Dose:** 500-1000 mcg
- **When:** With breakfast, daily (or 2500 mcg once weekly).
- **Why:** B12 exists almost exclusively in animal foods. On pure veg you WILL become deficient without it, and deficiency means fatigue, nerve problems and stalled recovery. This is the single most important supplement for a vegetarian — more important than whey.

### Vitamin D3 (essential)

- **Dose:** 2000 IU
- **When:** With a meal containing fat — your peanut butter breakfast or almond snack.
- **Why:** You are indoors coding from 9 to 5. Low D3 suppresses testosterone, mood and recovery. Fat-soluble, so it needs to ride along with dietary fat to absorb.

### Algal Omega-3 (DHA + EPA) (optional)

- **Dose:** 500 mg combined DHA+EPA
- **When:** With lunch.
- **Why:** Flax and walnuts give you ALA, which converts to usable DHA/EPA at only about 5%. Algal oil is the vegetarian source of the real thing — it manages inflammation from 6 training days a week and protects joint health.

### Caffeine (black coffee) (optional)

- **Dose:** 150-200 mg — 1-2 cups
- **When:** 4:45 AM pre-gym, and the 5:00 PM block if you need it. Never after 6:00 PM.
- **Why:** Free performance in a deficit, and it blunts appetite. Hard cut-off at 6 PM — caffeine has a 6-hour half-life and your 10 PM sleep is load-bearing for the whole plan.

### Multivitamin (optional)

- **Dose:** 1 tablet
- **When:** With lunch.
- **Why:** Cheap insurance for iron, zinc and selenium, which run low on no-cook vegetarian eating. Optional if your diet is dialled in, but at ₹150/month it is not worth the risk.

## 10. The APT Correction Program

### What APT Is

Anterior Pelvic Tilt is your pelvis rotating forward and down at the front, like a bucket of water tipping and spilling out over your toes. Because your pelvis is the base your spine sits on, when it tips forward your lower back has to arch harder to keep you upright. That arch is what you see from the side, and what you feel as a stiff, achy lower back by 4 PM.

### Why You Have It

You sit for about 8 hours a day writing software. In a chair your hip flexors — mainly the psoas, which runs from your lumbar spine over the front of your hip to your thigh — are held at roughly half their length all day. Muscles adapt to the position you keep them in, so over years they shorten and their resting tone goes up.

At the same time your glutes do nothing for those 8 hours. They are sat on, not used. Muscles that are never asked to work become weak and, worse, your nervous system stops recruiting them properly — so even when you do stand up, your lower back does the job your glutes should be doing.

Your deep core, the muscles that hold your ribcage stacked over your pelvis, also switches off in a slumped chair. In APT your ribs flare upward while your pelvis tips forward, which leaves your lumbar spine squeezed between them in a permanent arch.

Then you train — and overhead presses, shoulder presses, lat pulldowns and tricep extensions all tempt you to arch your lower back to move more weight. Without deliberate cueing, the gym makes APT worse rather than better. That is why every exercise in this plan carries its own APT cue.

### What It Costs You

- Lower back ache that arrives in the afternoon and eases when you lie down
- A stomach that looks like it sticks out even once your body fat is low — the pelvis is tipping your abdomen forward, not fat
- Glutes that look flat from the side regardless of training
- Hip pinching at the bottom of squats and leg presses
- Hamstrings that feel permanently tight because the tilt holds them lengthened all day
- The thing most people miss: at 68 kg with a 12% body fat and an uncorrected tilt, you will still not look lean from the side. Posture is roughly half of how lean you appear.

### What To Expect, When

| Period | What happens |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1-2 | You learn what neutral feels like. Nothing visible changes yet. The wall test gap starts to close slightly. |
| Weeks 3-6 | Hip flexors genuinely begin to lengthen. Glutes start firing on their own in daily movement. Lower back ache reduces noticeably. |
| Week 7 | Deload. Mobility only. Things often feel best this week, which tells you fatigue was masking progress. |
| Weeks 8-11 | The biggest visible change. Loaded hip thrusts and long RKC planks make the corrected position strong enough to hold all day without thinking. |
| Weeks 12-13 | The new posture becomes your default. Side-on photos against week 1 show the difference clearly — this is the part that surprises people. |

### If You Only Do Six Things

1. Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch, 2 x 60 sec per side, every night. Tuck the tailbone first — always.
2. Desk reset every 45 minutes during work hours. Stand, squeeze, 20 steps.
3. Hip thrusts or glute bridges on every gym day, progressively loaded.
4. 90/90 breathing, 3 rounds, every day.
5. Never arch your lower back to move more weight. Not once, on any exercise.
6. Do not sleep face-down.

### Self-Assessment

#### Wall Test *(Every 2 weeks)*

**Needs:** A flat wall

1. Stand with your heels about 10 cm from a wall.
2. Put your head, shoulder blades and glutes all in contact with the wall. Stand the way you normally stand — do not correct yourself.
3. Slide one flat hand into the gap between your lower back and the wall.

| Result | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ✓ Hand slides in snugly, barely any room | Neutral pelvis. This is the target. |
| ✗ Hand slides in with room to spare | Mild to moderate APT. |
| ✗ You can fit a whole fist, or your hand plus a gap | Significant APT — this is most likely your week-1 result. |

**Log as:** Record it as fingers of gap: 'snug', '1 hand', '1 hand + gap', or 'fist'.

*The single most useful test because it is objective, repeatable and takes five seconds. The gap closing fortnight by fortnight is your clearest evidence the program is working.*

#### Thomas Test *(Every 2 weeks)*

**Needs:** A bed or bench edge

1. Sit on the very edge of a bed or bench, right at the end.
2. Lie back while pulling both knees to your chest.
3. Keep the left knee hugged to your chest and let the right leg hang down freely, completely relaxed.
4. Look at where the hanging thigh and shin end up. Then swap sides.

| Result | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ✓ Back of the thigh rests flat on the bench and the shin hangs at about 80 degrees | Normal hip flexor length. |
| ✗ Thigh floats up off the bench | Tight psoas / iliacus — the main APT driver. |
| ✗ Thigh rests but the shin kicks forward toward straight | Tight rectus femoris — this is why the couch stretch with a bent knee is in the program. |

**Log as:** Record per side: 'thigh flat', 'thigh raised', or 'shin kicks out'.

*This tells you which structure is actually tight, so you know whether to prioritise the half-kneeling stretch (psoas) or the couch stretch (rectus femoris).*

#### ASIS / Pubic Bone Landmark Test *(Every 2 weeks)*

**Needs:** Nothing, plus a mirror

1. Stand relaxed and side-on to a mirror.
2. Find the bony point at the front of each hip — the ASIS. Place your index fingers on them.
3. Place your thumbs on your pubic bone at the front centre of your pelvis.
4. Your hands now make a triangle. Look at whether that triangle is vertical.

| Result | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ✓ The triangle is roughly vertical — fingers directly above thumbs | Neutral pelvis. |
| ✗ Fingers sit clearly in front of your thumbs | Anterior pelvic tilt, and the bigger the horizontal gap the bigger the tilt. |

**Log as:** Record as the rough forward distance: 'vertical', '2 cm forward', '4 cm forward'.

*The most direct measurement of the pelvis itself, since it reads bone landmarks rather than inferring from your back. Worth learning because you can run it anywhere in ten seconds.*

#### Standing Side Photo *(Weekly (photo), compared every 2 weeks)*

**Needs:** Phone, a plain wall, consistent light

1. Set your phone at hip height about 2 m away, strictly side-on.
2. Stand relaxed in your natural posture, arms by your sides, minimal clothing.
3. Take the photo without correcting your posture. This is critical — fixing your stance for the camera destroys the only objective record you have.
4. Same spot, same light, same time of day, every week.

| Result | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ✓ Ribs stacked over pelvis, flat-ish lower back, glutes sitting under the torso | Neutral. |
| ✗ Visible lumbar curve, belly pushed forward, glutes pushed back | APT visible from the side. |

**Log as:** Keep the photos in a dated album. Compare week 1 against the current week.

*Posture changes far too slowly to notice day to day. Two photos eight weeks apart make a change obvious that you genuinely cannot see in the mirror each morning.*

### The Four-Step Protocol

The order never changes: you cannot strengthen a muscle into a position a tight muscle will not let it reach, and you cannot hold a position your brain does not default to.

#### Step 1: Release

**Goal:** Lower the resting tone of the tight structures so the stretch that follows can actually work.

Releasing does not permanently lengthen anything — anyone who tells you five minutes of foam rolling changed their muscle length is mistaken. What it does do is reduce the protective tone in an overworked muscle for the next 20-30 minutes, which is a window where stretching is far more effective. That is the only reason it comes first.

| Drill | Dose | Cue | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foam roll quads & hip flexors | 90-120 sec per side | Slow. Stop on tender spots and breathe out for 20-30 seconds until they ease. | Day 4, Day 7, and any day your hips feel locked |
| Foam roll glutes | 90 sec per side | Sit on the roller with one ankle crossed over the opposite knee. | Day 7 |
| Foam roll mid & upper back | 60 sec | Never roll directly on your lower spine — in APT those erectors are already short and irritable, and grinding them makes them guard harder. | Day 2, Day 7 |

#### Step 2: Stretch

**Goal:** Lengthen the hip flexors and quads that are physically holding the front of your pelvis down.

The single most important technical point in this entire program: tuck your tailbone FIRST, squeeze the glute of the stretching side, and only then move. If you lean forward with an arched lower back you will feel a strong pull, but it is your lumbar spine compressing, not your psoas lengthening — and it actively makes the tilt worse. A correctly performed hip flexor stretch has a very small range of motion. If you can lunge a long way forward, your pelvis is not tucked.

| Drill | Dose | Cue | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch | 60 sec x 2-3 per side, daily | Tuck tailbone → squeeze kneeling-side glute → shift forward 2 inches. In that order, every time. | Every single day, gym or not |
| Couch stretch | 60 sec x 2 per side | Rear shin flat against a bench so the knee bends fully — this adds the rectus femoris. Build up to an upright torso over weeks. | Day 4, Day 6 |
| Standing quad stretch | 45 sec per side | Tuck the tailbone before you pull the heel in, or you will just arch and feel nothing useful. | Day 3, Day 6 |
| Seated hamstring stretch | 45 sec per side | Hinge from the hip with a flat back. Gently — in APT hamstrings are long-and-tight, not short-and-tight, so they need strengthening more than stretching. | Day 3, Day 4, Day 6, Day 7 |

#### Step 3: Strengthen

**Goal:** Build the glutes, hamstrings and deep core strong enough to hold the pelvis in neutral for 16 waking hours.

This is the half people skip, and it is why stretching alone never fixes APT. Stretching creates the room for a neutral pelvis; only strength holds it there. Your glutes pull the back of the pelvis down, your hamstrings assist, and your deep core keeps your ribs stacked over the top. All three have been switched off by years of sitting and need to be trained deliberately, not just used incidentally.

| Drill | Dose | Cue | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hip thrust | 3-4 x 12-15, loaded and progressive | Chin tucked, ribs down, stop at the straight line. Add weight every week — this is the strongest glute builder you have. | Day 2, Day 4, Day 6 |
| Glute bridge | 3 x 15-20 with a 2 sec hold | Flatten the lower back first, then lift from the glutes only. | Day 1, Day 3, Day 5 |
| RKC plank | 3-4 x 45-60 sec of maximum tension | Tailbone tucked hard, glutes and quads at 100%, elbows pulling toward toes. If you can hold 2 minutes you are not squeezing hard enough. | Day 2, Day 4, Day 5 |
| Deadbug | 3-4 x 12 per side | Lower back crushed flat into the floor. If it lifts a centimetre, shorten the range. | Day 1, Day 3, Day 4, Day 5 |
| Bird dog | 3 x 10 per side | Leg never goes above hip height — higher only comes from arching. | Day 2 |
| Reverse crunch | 3 x 15 | Roll the pelvis back; do not swing the legs. This is the literal opposite of a sit-up, which trains the hip flexors you are trying to calm down. | Day 4 |
| Side plank | 3 x 40 sec per side | Squeeze the bottom glute. Trains obliques and glute medius together to keep the pelvis level side to side. | Day 4 |
| Romanian deadlift | 3-4 x 10-12, loaded | The best hamstring and glute builder in the plan. Strong hamstrings pull the back of the pelvis down into neutral. | Day 3, Day 6 |
| Hip abduction | 3 x 15-20 | Glute medius stops the pelvis dropping and rotating when you walk. | Day 3, Day 6 |

#### Step 4: Re-pattern

**Goal:** Make the corrected position your unconscious default, so the other 23 hours stop undoing the one hour of work.

The step that decides whether any of this holds. You could have perfectly mobile hips and strong glutes and still stand in a tilt, because posture is a habit your nervous system runs without asking you. Twenty minutes of drills cannot beat sixteen hours of default. So every APT session ends by walking away still holding the position, and the desk resets through the day are what turn that into your new normal.

| Drill | Dose | Cue | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90/90 breathing | 3 x 5 breaths, 4 sec in / 8 sec out | The long exhale physically pulls your ribcage down over your pelvis. If you only ever did one drill, this would be it. | Day 4, Day 6, Day 7, and any evening you feel locked up |
| Cat-camel | 2 x 10-12 slow | Spend longer at the rounded, tailbone-tucked end — that is the end of the range your body has forgotten. | Day 4, Day 7 |
| Standing wall posture reset | 3 x 45 sec + 10 steps | The 10 steps after you leave the wall are the actual exercise. A hold followed by an immediate slump teaches nothing. | Day 4, Day 7, and once at your desk daily |
| Supine pelvic tilt | 3 x 15 with 3 sec holds | Memorise the sensation, then replicate about 30% of that tuck whenever you stand or walk. | Day 1, Day 3, Day 6, and daily at home |
| Desk reset | 60 sec every 45 min, 9 AM - 5 PM | Stand, 10 sec glute squeeze, 20 steps. Set a repeating timer — you will not remember on your own. | Every working hour of every working day |

#### Desk Reset — Every 45 min, 9 AM - 5 PM *(60 sec)*

The most important APT habit in the entire plan, and the one most likely to be skipped. Eight hours of sitting is the cause; this is the antidote, applied in small doses all day. Set a repeating timer — willpower will not remember.

| Item | Dose | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Stand up fully | Immediately | No finishing the thought first. Stand, then think. |

You cannot out-stretch a badly set up chair. Fix it once and it works for you all day.

| Screen at eye level | Raise the laptop | A low screen pulls your head and ribs forward, and your pelvis follows. |

Non-negotiable, every night, including rest days. Today's sitting shortened your hip flexors; this is where you give that back before you sleep.

Seven hours is a quarter of your day in one position. It is either helping or working against you.

### 13-Week APT Progression

#### Weeks 1-2 — Learn neutral

You cannot correct a position you cannot find. Everything this fortnight is about sensation, not load.

- Supine pelvic tilt, 3 x 15 with 3 sec holds — every single day, gym or not
- Deadbug, 3 x 12 per side, strictly within the range where your back stays flat
- Glute bridge, bodyweight, 3 x 20
- Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch, 60 sec x 2 per side, nightly
- Desk reset every 45 minutes — build the timer habit now, it is the one that carries the whole plan

**Test:** Run all 4 self-tests and write the results down. This is your baseline and you will not get another chance at it.

#### Weeks 3-6 — Full protocol

All four steps daily. Begin adding load to the glute work.

- Full Release → Stretch → Strengthen → Re-pattern order on every gym day
- Hip thrust becomes loaded and progressive — add weight weekly
- RKC plank, 3 x 45-60 sec of genuine maximum tension
- Couch stretch introduced on Day 4 and Day 6, leaning forward at first
- 90/90 breathing, 3 rounds, daily
- Night routine every night without exception

**Test:** Re-test on weeks 3 and 5. The wall test gap should be measurably smaller by week 5.

#### Week 7 — Deload — mobility only

Release and stretch only. Let the strength work recover.

- All release and stretch drills daily, full hold times
- Drop the loaded hip thrusts and the long planks entirely this week
- Keep pelvic tilts and 90/90 breathing — they are not fatiguing
- Keep every desk reset — the habit never deloads

**Test:** Re-test on week 7. Things often feel their best this week, which tells you fatigue had been masking your progress.

#### Weeks 8-11 — Load the correction

The block where visible posture change happens. Strong under load is what holds a position for 16 hours.

- Hip thrust heavy, 4 x 12-15, beating last week's load
- RKC plank, 4 x 60 sec
- Couch stretch progressed toward a fully upright torso
- Pallof press and side planks added for anti-rotation and lateral control
- Walk the corrected posture deliberately on every treadmill and evening walk

**Test:** Re-test on weeks 9 and 11. By week 11 the wall test should be close to snug.

#### Weeks 12-13 — Make it automatic

Re-patterning above all. The drills should now feel easy; the point is that you no longer think about them.

- Maintain strength work — do not chase new PRs in the deepest deficit
- Wall reset plus 10 steps, three times daily
- 90/90 breathing nightly
- Full stretch routine nightly
- Consciously check your standing posture several times a day until you stop needing to

**Test:** Final re-test on week 13, plus the side-by-side photo against week 1. That comparison is the real result.

### When The Scale Stops Moving

1. A stall is only a stall after 10 days of a flat weekly average — not after one bad morning. Weigh daily, judge the 7-day average.
2. First, audit honesty: are you actually hitting the kcal and protein numbers, including weekend PG meals and the oil in them? 9 times out of 10 the plan is not broken, the logging is.
3. Second, add 1,500 steps a day before you cut any food. Movement is cheaper than hunger.
4. Third, if still flat after 10 more days, cut 100 kcal from carbs only. Never cut protein, never cut below 1,600 kcal on this plan.
5. Never stack cuts. One change, 10 days, re-measure.
